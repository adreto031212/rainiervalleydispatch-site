The latest in Rainier Beach

November 12, 2026

The rebuild of Rainier Beach High School is in its last phase. The district's project page lists November 2026 for finishing the athletic fields, the fifth phase; the performing arts center was set to be ready for the first day of school, September 2. Phased construction began in April 2022 and is scheduled to wrap up in December. Seattle Public Schools SOLD:Rainier Beach

November 5, 2026

Pritchard Island Beach sits north of Beer Sheva Park, with tall cottonwoods and a view of Seward Park to the north and Mercer Island to the east. A gravel path winds through more than four acres of Pritchard Wetland, planted only with species native to western Washington. Pritchard Island Beach

October 29, 2026

Kubota Garden is 20 acres of hills, streams, waterfalls, ponds and bridges that the city bought in 1987 from the estate of landscaper Fujitaro Kubota, who started his display garden there in 1927. More than 65,000 people visit a year; it is open every day from sunrise to sundown. Seattle Parks · Kubota Garden

October 22, 2026

Pritchard Island Beach sits north of Beer Sheva Park, with tall cottonwoods and a view of Seward Park to the north and Mercer Island to the east. Just south, a gravel path from the parking lot winds through more than four acres of Pritchard Wetland, planted only with species native to western Washington wetlands; watch for herons and muskrats in Pritchard Pond. Pritchard Island Beach

October 17, 2026

The new skatepark at Rainier Beach Playfield, 4707 South Cloverdale Street, opened September 21, with street and transition terrain and a mural by Myron Curry; it was paid for with $850,000 from the King County Parks Levy. Next Saturday, October 24, Rainier Beach Community Center, 8825 Rainier Avenue South, holds its Harvest Fest from 2 to 5 PM, for ages 5 to 18. Skatepark · Fall events

Questions people ask

Is this a real estate newsletter?

No. It is a Rainier Valley newsletter that happens to know the housing market. Three of the four sections are about living here. The fourth tells you what homes are selling for, because in Rainier Valley that is news too.

Who writes it?

Neighborhood Press LLC, an independent publisher. Every item comes from Rainier Valley's own public record (council agendas, permit notices, school district and transit updates, parks calendars) and links its source. It is researched and written with AI tools, and an editor reads every issue before it goes out.

How often, and is it really free?

Once a week, Thursday at 6 AM, about five minutes to read. Free, no paywall, unsubscribe in one click at the bottom of any issue.

Who owns it?

Neighborhood Press LLC, an independent publishing company. The newsletter is free, and no sponsor buys coverage. Our standards.

What happens with my address?

It is used for one thing: a monthly estimate of what your home would sell for today. It is shared with a local real estate brokerage under the same ownership as our publisher, which may contact you about your home, and is never sold or posted. Remove it any time by replying to any issue.
