The latest in North Beacon Hill

November 12, 2026

The Beacon Hill branch library, 2821 Beacon Avenue South, has a canopied stone entry with sheltered seating and bike racks, neighborhood writing set into quarry rocks outside, and "Dream Ship," a boat sculpture over the plaza that moves with the wind. It is open until 8 PM on Wednesdays and Thursdays. Beacon Hill branch SOLD:North Beacon Hill

November 5, 2026

Jefferson Park is the sixth largest park in the city, with views of the Duwamish River, downtown and the Olympics from an Olmsted-inspired path system on the covered reservoirs. It holds the Bill Wright Golf Complex, the lawn bowling greens, a skatepark and Beacon Mountain. Jefferson Park

October 29, 2026

Dr. Jose Rizal Park, 1007 12th Avenue South, looks out over south downtown and Elliott Bay. Its 4-acre off-leash area at the north end is reached by a long stairway, or step-free from the bike trail that connects to the Mountains to Sound Greenway; picnic tables, restrooms and a small play area are to the south. Dr. Jose Rizal Park

October 22, 2026

Jefferson Park is the sixth largest park in the city, with views of the Duwamish River, downtown and the Olympics from an Olmsted-inspired path system on the covered reservoirs. It holds the Bill Wright Golf Complex, renamed in 2024 for the golfer who learned the game there and won the 1959 US Amateur Public Links Championship, plus the lawn bowling greens, a skatepark and Beacon Mountain. The lawn bowling clubhouse closes on weekends into early November for water-pipe work. Jefferson Park

October 17, 2026

The Jefferson Park Lawn Bowling Club building closes for three weekends between mid-October and early November while Parks replaces water pipe to fix discolored water, possibly with a new booster pump, water heater and filter. Jefferson Community Center, 3801 Beacon Avenue South, holds a free Halloween carnival Saturday, October 24, 1 to 4:30 PM. Lawn bowling building · Fall events

Questions people ask

Is this a real estate newsletter?

No. It is a Rainier Valley newsletter that happens to know the housing market. Three of the four sections are about living here. The fourth tells you what homes are selling for, because in Rainier Valley that is news too.

Who writes it?

Neighborhood Press LLC, an independent publisher. Every item comes from Rainier Valley's own public record (council agendas, permit notices, school district and transit updates, parks calendars) and links its source. It is researched and written with AI tools, and an editor reads every issue before it goes out.

How often, and is it really free?

Once a week, Thursday at 6 AM, about five minutes to read. Free, no paywall, unsubscribe in one click at the bottom of any issue.

Who owns it?

Neighborhood Press LLC, an independent publishing company. The newsletter is free, and no sponsor buys coverage. Our standards.

What happens with my address?

It is used for one thing: a monthly estimate of what your home would sell for today. It is shared with a local real estate brokerage under the same ownership as our publisher, which may contact you about your home, and is never sold or posted. Remove it any time by replying to any issue.
