The latest in Brighton

November 12, 2026

At 7500 Martin Luther King Jr. Way South, a few blocks from the Othello station, a land use application filed October 5 (3044123-LU) would remove three underground fuel storage tanks, two of 10,000 gallons and one of 8,000, and dig out 1,400 cubic yards of soil, backfilled with clean material. A related construction permit covers removing petroleum-contaminated material and restoring the surface. Seattle permit records SOLD:Brighton

November 5, 2026

Brighton Playfield, 6000 39th Avenue South, was bought by the city in 1913 and is named for the original town site, Brighton Beach, after the English seaport. Brighton Playfield

October 29, 2026

Seattle Public Utilities is building a planted stormwater area where South Kenyon Street meets the Chief Sealth Trail, east of Wing Luke Elementary along 39th Avenue South. Special soil and deep-rooted plants will filter runoff from about 25 acres uphill before it reaches Lake Washington; the plan adds seating, a stairway and public artwork by RYAN! Feddersen. SPU lists 2026 for construction. SPU

October 22, 2026

Graham Street station, the planned street-level 1 Line stop near MLK Jr. Way South and South Graham Street, between Columbia City and Othello stations, is now fully funded through planning, design and construction under the updated system plan. Next step: preliminary engineering and the environmental documents, which Sound Transit expects to publish in early 2027. Sound Transit

October 17, 2026

Othello Playground, 4351 South Othello Street, is getting a fenced off-leash area in the lawn at its southwest corner, with seating and a planted stormwater area. Parks plans to bid the work in late 2026, build from late 2026 into early 2027 and open it in fall 2027. Questions people ask

Is this a real estate newsletter?

No. It is a Rainier Valley newsletter that happens to know the housing market. Three of the four sections are about living here. The fourth tells you what homes are selling for, because in Rainier Valley that is news too.

Who writes it?

Neighborhood Press LLC, an independent publisher. Every item comes from Rainier Valley's own public record (council agendas, permit notices, school district and transit updates, parks calendars) and links its source. It is researched and written with AI tools, and an editor reads every issue before it goes out.

How often, and is it really free?

Once a week, Thursday at 6 AM, about five minutes to read. Free, no paywall, unsubscribe in one click at the bottom of any issue.

Who owns it?

Neighborhood Press LLC, an independent publishing company. The newsletter is free, and no sponsor buys coverage. Our standards.

What happens with my address?

It is used for one thing: a monthly estimate of what your home would sell for today. It goes to a local real estate brokerage under the same ownership as our publisher only if you tick the sharing box on the form; that brokerage may then contact you about your home. Leave the box empty and it stays with us. Never sold or posted. Remove it any time by replying to any issue.
